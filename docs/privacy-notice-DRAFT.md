# What We Keep, and Why

**The Rampant Club — Privacy Notice**
Version 1.0 (draft) · [DATE]

---

Most clubs give you a privacy notice written by someone who hopes you won't read it. This one is
written on the assumption that you will, and that you'd rather know.

The short version: we keep a great deal about you. We keep it because a club that remembers is a
better club than one that doesn't, and because the alternative — asking you the same questions every
visit — is worse service dressed as discretion. None of it is sold, none of it is shared with other
businesses, and you can see all of it whenever you like.

The longer version is below, including the parts that might give you pause. There are a few. We'd
rather you read them here than discover them later.

## What we keep

### Who you are
Your name, the name we actually call you, your membership number, your contact details, your date of
birth, your membership tier and dates, and who introduced you.

### What you like — and how sure we are
The club keeps a register of your preferences: the drams you've enjoyed, the ones you've politely
declined, how you take your water, where you prefer to sit, whether you'd rather be introduced or
left alone. Some you'll have told us. Much is observed by staff and written down afterwards.

Three things about that register worth stating plainly:

- **It sometimes records your words.** Where what you said matters, staff note the phrase itself, not
  a paraphrase.
- **It is scored.** Each entry carries a confidence level, and that confidence **decays** — a taste
  noted once, two years ago, is held more loosely than one confirmed on your last four visits. The
  system is built to forget what has stopped being true.
- **It notices when you contradict yourself.** If you tell us one thing and later do another, that
  conflict is flagged rather than silently overwritten, so a member of staff can work out which is
  current. Nobody is judging the inconsistency; we simply don't want to serve you last year's answer.

### What happens when you're here
Your visits: the date, the room, how long you stayed, who looked after you, and a written note about
how the evening went. **That note includes an impression of your mood** — whether you seemed
celebratory, tired, or would rather not be approached. It exists so the next person looking after you
starts where the last one left off.

Also: your card taps at the kiosks, what you ordered and consumed, what you spent, guests you brought,
bottles you keep in a locker here, and gifts the club has sent you, including what they cost us.

### What you tell us and make
Concierge requests, direct messages, anything you post in The Snug and the reactions to it, tasting
notes you write, bottles you save, events you sign up for, photographs you contribute, and members
you have chosen to block.

### What the club works out about you
Two things are **derived**, not told to us:

- **Your palate.** Your tasting notes and preferences are combined into a flavour profile, which is
  what the radar on your portal and the kiosk shows you.
- **Who you might get on with.** That profile is compared with other members' to suggest
  introductions. **You are opted out of this by default** and nothing is suggested to anyone unless
  you turn it on.

### What you pay
Dues, top-ups, receipts and your account balance. **Card numbers are never stored by us** — payment
details are handled by our payment provider.

## The parts we'd rather tell you about than have you find

**Staff read a brief before you arrive.** When you're expected, whoever is looking after you sees a
short summary: your name, your preferences, anything outstanding from last time, and anything they
should know to make the evening go well. It exists so that good service looks like attentiveness
rather than interrogation. It is read backstage and never displayed where you or other members can
see it.

**We write down when things go wrong.** If you've had a poor evening, raised a complaint, or simply
seemed unhappy, a member of staff records it — what happened and what was done about it. We keep it
because the alternative, a complaint that vanishes when the person who heard it goes off shift, is
how clubs fail their members quietly. It is held in confidence and used to put things right.

**Staff write up the evening, and a computer reads it.** At the end of a shift a member of staff
writes a plain-English account of how the evening went. That account names members and describes what
happened. **It is then processed by an artificial-intelligence service — Anthropic's Claude — which
reads it and proposes structured entries** for the records described above. A member of staff reviews
and accepts each proposal; nothing is written to your record by the machine alone.

We tell you this because "we use AI" is usually where a notice stops being honest. The specifics:
the narrative leaves our systems, goes to Anthropic to be read, and comes back as proposed rows. It
is not used to train anyone's model. [CONFIRM CONTRACTUAL POSITION WITH ANTHROPIC AND TNJ LAW.]

**Members and staff are shown different things.** The screen you sign into at the bar shows your own
name, the room, what's on, and your palate. It deliberately does not show the scored preference
register, the visit notes, the mood impressions, or the service record. Those are working tools for
staff. You are entitled to see all of it — see below — but by asking us, not by finding it on a
tablet in a public room.

**Management reporting.** Summaries of how the club is performing are produced and sent by email to
management and the club's advisers. These are largely aggregate — visits, new members, revenue — but
may include member-level detail where it matters. [CONFIRM RECIPIENTS AND WHETHER NAMES APPEAR.]

## Before you were a member

The club keeps records on people it is considering, or has considered, for membership: name, contact
details, who introduced them and how they know them, the stage they reached, notes from conversations
and interviews, and the outcome.

**If you were introduced to the club and never joined, we still hold this.** You have the same rights
over it as a member does — see it, correct it, or have it deleted — and you can exercise them at

running the club.

to other businesses — including those connected to the club by ownership or supply.

While you are a member, and for [RETENTION PERIOD] after your membership ends.

Preference and visit records are deleted on request at any time, including while you remain a member.

## What you can ask us to do

At any time, and without explaining why:

- **See everything** we hold about you — including the preference register, the visit notes, the mood
  impressions, and the service record. Ask and we'll produce it.
- **Correct** anything that's wrong.
- **Delete** what you'd rather we didn't keep, subject only to records we're legally required to retain.
- **Withdraw your consent** — to marketing, to introductions, or to any processing not required to
  provide your membership.
- **Object** to something specific, or ask us to stop a particular kind of processing.
- **Complain**, to us first, and to the relevant Vietnamese authority if we haven't put it right.

Write to [PRIVACY CONTACT EMAIL]. We'll respond within [RESPONSE PERIOD].

## Marketing

Separate, optional, and off unless you turn it on. Declining costs you nothing — you'll still hear
about anything affecting your membership, because that isn't marketing. You can withdraw at any time
without affecting anything else.

---

## When this changes

We'll tell you, and we'll ask you to look at the new version next time you sign in. Every version is
kept, along with the date you agreed to it, so there is never a question about which terms applied
when.

## NOTES FOR LACHLAN — not part of the notice

**This is a draft for TNJ Law to confirm, not to write.** I'm not a lawyer and this isn't legal
advice. Everything described above was checked against the actual database rather than assumed.

**Four things I added that the earlier draft didn't cover, all of them material:**

1. **The AI processing.** Shift narratives naming members are sent to Anthropic for extraction. This
   is a cross-border transfer of member data to an automated system, and it was entirely absent. It
   is the single most likely thing to matter to a regulator and to a member.
2. **Prospects.** ~199 people, most with no relationship to the club and no consent, including who
   introduced them and interview notes. Legally the most exposed processing here.
3. **What visits actually record** — an impression of your mood, not just attendance.
4. **The contradiction flag** on preferences, and the fact that the register stores verbatim phrasing.

**Also now named:** lockers, guest visits, gifts and their cost, blocks, consumption, card taps,
derived palate and palate-matching (opt-out by default), and management reporting by email.

**Ask TNJ Law specifically about:**
- Lawful basis for observed (not volunteered) preference data
- Whether a member's right of access extends to staff notes about them, and whether staff identities
  are redacted
- Prospect data: basis, proactive notification, retention limit
- The AI processing: whether it needs separate consent, and the cross-border position
- Retention periods, left blank deliberately

**Biometrics:** if entrance facial recognition is ever deployed it cannot ride on this notice.
Separate consent, separate basis. Worth asking now rather than after the hardware is installed.

**Placeholders:** registered address and ERN, privacy contact email, processor regions, retention
period, response period, date.
